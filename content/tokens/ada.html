What is Cardano?

Cardano (ADA) is a third-generation blockchain platform founded by Charles Hoskinson, one of Ethereum's co-founders. Launched in 2017, Cardano distinguishes itself through its research-driven approach, with all developments peer-reviewed by academics before implementation.

How Cardano Works

Cardano uses Ouroboros, the first provably secure Proof of Stake protocol developed through peer-reviewed research. The platform is built in layers: the Cardano Settlement Layer (CSL) handles ADA transactions, while the Cardano Computation Layer (CCL) handles smart contracts.

Key Features

  • Peer-Reviewed: Academic research underpins all development
  • Ouroboros: Provably secure PoS consensus
  • Layered Architecture: Separation of concerns for flexibility
  • Native Tokens: No smart contracts needed for token creation
  • Sustainability: Treasury system for ongoing development

Tokenomics

  • Maximum Supply: 45,000,000,000 ADA
  • Block Time: ~20 seconds
  • Staking Rewards: ~4-5% APY
  • Treasury: 20% of rewards fund development
  • Consensus: Ouroboros Proof of Stake

Use Cases

  • Identity verification solutions
  • Supply chain tracking
  • Educational credentials
  • DeFi applications
  • Governance and voting systems

Ecosystem

Cardano's ecosystem has grown significantly with the introduction of smart contracts through the Alonzo upgrade. Projects span DeFi, NFTs, and real-world applications, with a particular focus on developing markets in Africa and emerging economies.
